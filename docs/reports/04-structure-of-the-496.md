# Structure of the record 496

This document explains what the record 496-point set *is*, structurally. Its central claim is that
the record lives on a **Turyn √2E8³ decomposition** of the Leech lattice Λ, that its 496 points are
all of one block-support type ("duads"), and that the independence constraints in that picture are
purely combinatorial: matchings in 45 8×8 rook grids indexed by a PG(3,2) of "channels". Within
that model the record count factors as

> **496 = 4 · (3·12 + 9·8 + 2·8).**

Everything below is either **VERIFIED** (an exact computation with a quoted `RESULT` line — see
§14) or explicitly **CONJECTURED** (interpretation, or a claim supported only by search evidence).
Where an earlier structural analysis was wrong, the corrected version is stated inline and
collected in §12. Negative results are kept as results: §11.3 lists every structural hypothesis
that was tested and refuted.

Companion documents: `01-lattice-and-verification.md` (Golay/Leech construction, the 196560
minimal vectors, canonical order, verifiers), `02-upper-bounds.md` (LP/SDP bounds),

## 0. Notation and local vocabulary

**Scaling.** Minimal vectors of Λ are carried in the √8-integer scaling: |v|² = 32 (standard
norm 4). Inner products between minimal vectors take values in {±32, ±16, ±8, 0}. Standard-norm
quantities are marked "std" where both appear.

**Conflict graph G.** Vertices are the 196560 minimal vectors; two are adjacent when their inner
product is +16 (angle 60°). The record set S ⊂ Λ is an independent set of G of size 496; it is
antipodal, so it also avoids −16, and the two notions coincide on it. Gram off-diagonal
multiset of S: `{−32: 248, −8: 37504, 0: 47504, 8: 37504}`.

**Tightness.** For a minimal vector v ∉ S, its *tightness* is the number of members of S adjacent
to it in G (i.e. at inner product +16); "free" means tightness 0. S has no vertex of tightness
1, 2 or 3, and exactly **80 vertices of tightness 4** — the global minimum. These 80 are the
objects of §5. Since S is antipodal, each such vertex also has 4 members of S at −16.

**Blocks.** The Turyn decomposition is an orthogonal splitting R²⁴ = B₁ ⊥ B₂ ⊥ B₃ into three
8-dimensional blocks with Λ ∩ Bᵢ ≅ √2E8 (§1). Write s = s₁ + s₂ + s₃ for the block components of
a vector and nᵢ = |sᵢ|².

**Monads, duads, triads.** A minimal vector is a **monad** if its norm sits in one block
(32,0,0), a **duad** if it splits 16+16 over two blocks, a **triad** if it splits 16+8+8. These
are the only three possibilities (§2), with 720 / 11520 / 184320 vectors respectively.

**Pairs.** "Pair 0" = {B₂,B₃}, "pair 1" = {B₁,B₃}, "pair 2" = {B₁,B₂}. A duad is supported on
exactly one pair.

**Orbits.** The stabiliser of S in Co₀ is the group 2³ of blockwise sign changes (§1). Its orbits
on duads are the quadruples {±x, ±y} where x, y are the two block components — 2880 orbits of size
4 in total. Monads fall into 360 orbits of size 2. S is a union of 124 duad orbits. These 2880
size-4 orbits are exactly the "usable orbits" of the exhaustive 2³-invariant orbit search
(`03-search-for-497.md`); the 360 size-2 orbits are the monad pairs.

**Channels.** For a duad orbit {±x, ±y} the two norm-64 vectors x+y and x−y are congruent
mod 2Λ; their common class in Λ/2Λ is a type-4 class (a Conway cross) called the orbit's
**channel**. Each ordered pair of blocks shares exactly 15 channels, the *same* 15 for all three
pairs, and these are precisely the 15 nonzero elements of a 4-dimensional subspace T ⊂ Λ/2Λ —
i.e. the points of a **PG(3,2)** (§4).

**Rook grids and cells.** Within a block, a channel class consists of 8 ± pairs of norm-64 vectors
forming an orthogonal frame. So a (pair, channel) **cell** is an 8×8 **rook grid**: rows = the 8
± pairs of the channel frame in the first block, columns = the same in the second, and the 64
cells of the grid are the 64 available duad orbits. There are 3 × 15 = **45 cells**, 45 × 64 =
2880 orbits ✓. Two orbits that share a row (or column) conflict; distinct rows and columns give
inner product 0. Hence independence within one cell means a **partial permutation matrix** (rook
placement, ≤ 8 orbits), and independence within one channel across all three pairs means a
**matching in K₈,₈,₈** (≤ 12 orbits).

**Profile and wiring.** The **profile** of a duad set is the 45 integers "how many orbits in each
cell"; the **wiring** is which particular matchings realise them. §6 shows the profile is constant
across the entire known 496-plateau; only the wiring moves.

### 4.2 Channels

## 5. The 80 tightness-4 vertices as a channel object

**VERIFIED.** The 80 tightness-4 vertices (call this set T4) are **duads** (block-norm split
16+16), 48/16/16 over pairs 0/1/2, forming 40 antipodal pairs, mutually non-adjacent, with Gram
off-diagonal multiset `{−32: 40, −8: 640, 0: 1840, 8: 640}`. They are closed under
Stab_Co₀(S) = 2³ and decompose into **20 orbits of size 4**.

Their channel/cell table is startlingly small — **they occupy only the three frame channels**:

| channel | pair 0 | pair 1 | pair 2 | total orbits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cF2 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **12** — a full perfect K₈,₈,₈ matching = a complete 24-class frame |
| cF0 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 4 |
| cF1 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 4 |
| all 12 other channels | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Conflict law (VERIFIED).** A tightness-4 orbit conflicts with exactly those S-orbits that share
one of its two rook rows in the same channel. Every tightness-4 orbit conflicts with exactly 2
S-orbits, and the prediction "conflicts = shared rows" holds for all 20 orbits with no exception.
Conflicts in this structure are purely row collisions; **there are no cross-channel conflicts
anywhere in S ∪ T4**.

So: the tightness-4 vertices are precisely the **complementary matching of frame channel cF2**
plus the **pair-0 quarter of the alternative wirings of cF0 and cF1** — the quarter whose rows
collide least. Two collisions per orbit is the minimum any outside duad can have here, which is
exactly why the global minimum tightness is 4 = 2 orbits × 2 vectors at inner product +16.

### 6.2 The profile is the invariant, not the set

**Deliverable statement.** *The tightness-4 vertices of the record set are the alternative channel
wirings — the complementary cF2 matching plus the pair-0 quarters of the alternative {cF0,c07} and
{cF1,c04} wirings; the (80,80) moves are complete rewirings of one σ-pair of channels with the cell

The cell profile (4,4,4 / one-pair 8-permutations / 4+4) is an invariant of the **entire
64-plateau**, not merely of S: local search never changes *how much* each cell carries, only
*which* matching realises it. **Any >496 duad set must therefore break the profile itself.** The
open [496, 720] gap of the Turyn-slice model (`03-search-for-497.md`) is exactly the question of
which profiles are realisable.

## 11. Why 496 — the unified account

### 11.1 The verified chain

1. Fix the Turyn decomposition R²⁴ = B₁ ⊥ B₂ ⊥ B₃, Λ ∩ Bᵢ = √2E8, [Λ : √2E8³] = 2¹² (§1). The
   196560 minimal vectors split 720 monads / 11520 duads / 184320 triads (§2).
2. S and all 64 plateau sets are **pure duad sets on ONE such decomposition**, with support split
   (208, 144, 144) (§2). Duads organise into 45 (pair, channel) rook cells of 64 orbits each;
   independence within a channel is a K₈,₈,₈ matching (≤ 12 orbits), whence the ceiling
   15 × 12 × 4 = **720** — the certified upper bound for the 2³-invariant (Turyn-slice) problem
   (`03-search-for-497.md`).
3. S's profile: 3 channels perfectly matched (= the three frames), 9 channels at one-pair
   8-permutations, 2 at 4+4, 1 empty: **496 = 4·(3·12 + 9·8 + 2·8)** (§4.3). The profile is
   invariant across the whole plateau (§6). The three perfect-matching channels are the three
   frames, and the (12,12) and (80,80) plateau moves and the (48,48) frame swap all move *within*
   the duad set of this one decomposition — verified over all 64 certified sets.
4. Everything "local" about the record is now channel-geometric: minimum tightness 4 = the minimal
   row-collision count of an outside duad (§5); the plateau = σ-pair rewirings (§6); the maximal
   cliques of G that dominate the clique census are triad objects invisible to the duad
   model (§9).
5. Bounds: two-point methods are blind (LP = 850.9 with or without −16; the Hoffman bound on the
   single-pair duad graph is ≈ 282 ≫ 208); the three-point SDP gives 837; clique cuts cannot bite
   (ω = 24 ≪ 235). Within the duad model every fast relaxation stalls at 720, because the flat
   profile (4 orbits per cell) satisfies all quickly-provable caps.
6. Constraints "partial permutation per cell" and "matching per channel" alone cap a duad set at
   15 × 12 = 180 orbits = 720 vectors; the actual optimum under the *cross-channel* constraints is
   (at least, and by all searches exactly) 124 orbits.

### 11.2 Conjectures

* **CONJECTURED**: 496 is the maximum independent duad set on a Turyn frame. This is exactly the
  open [496, 720] gap of the Turyn-slice model, i.e. the "maximum 2³-invariant set ≥ 496" question
  restricted to its only usable orbits. Iterated local search, branch and bound (1.6M nodes) and
  MILP all failed to beat 496; none proved optimality. A set of SAT decision instances aimed at
  closing parts of this gap had been written and launched but had not returned at the time of this
  analysis — nothing here rests on them. Their status is reported in `03-search-for-497.md`, and
  claims about that model should be read there, not inferred from this document.
* **CONJECTURED**: a >496 set, if one exists, is not 2³-invariant for any blockwise sign group —
  i.e. it either breaks the duad model's profile caps (a decidable finite question) or is not a
  duad set at all. Every other invariance class in the subgroup sweep caps below 402
  (`03-search-for-497.md`).
* **CONJECTURED (numerology rejected, with a VERIFIED ingredient)**: the coincidences
  496 = 2⁴·31 (a perfect number) and 248 = dim of the E8 Lie algebra are meaningless here. 31 does
  not divide |Co₀| = 8315553613086720000 (**VERIFIED**), so no 31-fold symmetry can exist; the
  natural factorisation is 496 = 4·124, not 16·31.

## 12. Corrections to the earlier structural analysis

1. **Trace bookkeeping in the stabiliser (corrected).** An earlier table asserted that two of the
   trace-+8 involutions ("b, ab") both have trace +8 and generate the third. Wrong: the three
   trace-+8 involutions satisfy **tᵢtⱼ = −tₖ**, so the products have trace **−8** and no two of
   them generate a Klein four-group containing the third. The group is unchanged
   ({±1} × ⟨t₁,t₂⟩ ≅ 2³ acting as the blockwise sign group); only the trace bookkeeping was wrong.
   The `RESULT` line records the corrected rule explicitly:
   `product_rule=t_i*t_j=-t_k (NOT t_k): the three trace+8 involutions do not form a Klein group`.
2. **The [24,7] octad code holds for only two of the three frames.** Frames 0 and 1 (channels cF0,
   cF1) give 21 distinct octads spanning a self-complementary [24,7] code; **frame 2 (channel cF2)
   gives 28 distinct octads spanning a [24,9] code** with a different weight distribution. Any
   statement of the form "the frame octad masks span a [24,7] code" is correct for two frames and
   false for the third. None of the three is the Golay code. The same asymmetry shows up in §3:
   frames 0 and 1 generate sublattices with Smith form [2⁴, 4¹⁰], frame 2 gives [4¹²], so frame 2
   is not Smith-equivalent to the other two.
3. **The empty channel is inside the span of the used channels (clarification).** The earlier
   phrasing "the one globally empty channel is the unique nonzero element of T outside the span
   structure of the 14 used" is loose and invites the wrong reading. The exact, verified statement
   is that the **XOR-stabiliser of the 14 used crosses is {0, cE}**. cE is *not* outside the span
   of used channels: **cF0 ⊕ cF1 = cE**, and ⟨cF0, cF1, cF2⟩ is a 3-dimensional subspace of T that
   contains cE.
4. **"Nine perfect channels" and related duad-model optimality claims are not settled here.** This
   document proves only the structural constraints (partial permutation per cell, matching per
   channel, ceiling 720) and reports 496 as the best value any search has reached. The status of
   the finer feasibility questions inside that model — including how many channels can be
   simultaneously perfect — belongs to `03-search-for-497.md`, where solver *timeouts* must not be
   read as infeasibility proofs. Nothing in this document depends on those questions being closed.
5. **The census families are reconstructed, not logged.** The GPU runs did not record their random
   Co₀ seed elements, so families are recovered by overlap clustering, and families absent from the
   dumps are covered by fresh random images that are statistically equivalent stand-ins rather than
   the actual seeds (§8).
6. **Wirings are not all equivalent.** It is tempting to say that, since the profile is invariant
   across the plateau, wirings are interchangeable. They are interchangeable for the pair
   statistics a template-consuming construction would look at *within one fingerprint class*, but
   the 64 plateau sets fall into **5 distinct tightness fingerprint classes**, so the fingerprint
   is a genuine wiring invariant and should be exposed rather than averaged away.

## 13. Where this leaves the search, the bounds, and construction

### 13.1 Search track

The entire 496-plateau explored so far is **one Turyn frame's duad structure**. Two concrete
directions follow.

* **(a) Search in the rook/channel model directly.** The 45-cell model is a 2880-orbit exact
  problem with heavy structure: the per-cell and per-channel constraints shrink the space
  enormously compared with a naive orbit branch-and-bound. An exact answer to the conjecture of
  §11.2 may be within reach of a SAT/ILP encoding over 124-orbit configurations.
* **(b) Other Turyn frames give nothing new by themselves.** Co₀ is transitive on Turyn frames, so
  any other frame gives an isomorphic structure. Hence a >496 set, if it exists, either beats 496
  *within* the duad model (decidable by (a)) or is not stabilised by any blockwise 2³ — consistent
  with the finding that every other subgroup family caps below 402.

### 13.2 Bounds track

No help from two-point relaxations (§10). The structural target for a three-point or exact
approach is the duad model of (a): if 496 is proven optimal there, the remaining question is
whether a maximum independent set must have a 2³ of this class at all.

### 13.3 Hooks for a construction method that consumes 496-sets

1. **The profile, not the set, is the object.** A 496-set = a realisable profile (how many orbits
   per cell) + a wiring (which matchings). Profiles are tiny discrete data — 45 small integers with
   PG(3,2) symmetry, the model's automorphism group acting on (channels, pairs) as GL(4,2) × S₃ —
   and wirings are cheap to enumerate once a profile is fixed. A method that consumes 496-sets can
   consume *profiles + wirings* instead. Caveat from §12.6: the 64 plateau sets fall into 5
   tightness fingerprint classes, so wirings are not all equivalent and the fingerprint is worth
   exposing to the method.
2. **Disjointness is wiring-level.** The known disjoint families in dimension 31 (42 disjoint
   496-sets; see `05-lifting-template-and-families.md`) come from powers of a single automorphism.
   The channel model offers a second mechanism: two duad sets on the same Turyn frame with
   row-disjoint wirings are automatically conflict-light. But the 720-orbit duad space holds at
   most 720/496 < 2 disjoint full sets, so **cross-frame** disjointness (different Turyn
   decompositions, on which Co₀ is transitive) remains the productive direction. The profile
   language transfers verbatim to any frame.
3. **The empty channel is an affine anomaly.** S wastes channel cE entirely and half of c04 and
   c07, and is nevertheless maximal. Whether a *different* profile — for instance one that uses cE
   at the cost of two 8-permutation channels — reaches 497 is a clean, self-contained, finite
   combinatorial problem: maximum independent orbit set in 45 rook cells under the verified
   conflict rules, currently known to lie in [496, 720].
4. **Triads are the unexplored stratum.** Every record-class object so far is duad-pure, and the
   conflict graph's big cliques are triad-pure (§9). A construction that mixes strata must manage
   duad–triad conflicts that the current model never sees. The entry points are the block-pattern
   census (720 / 11520 / 184320) and the mod-2Λ membership test of §3 (v ∈ ⟨S⟩ iff class(v) lies in
   a fixed 20-dimensional subspace of Λ/2Λ).
