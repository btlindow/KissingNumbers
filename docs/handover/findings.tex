\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Let $C$ be the $196560$ minimal vectors of the Leech lattice $\Lam$, and call
$S \subseteq C$ \emph{$60^\circ$-free} if no two of its members subtend an angle of
$60^\circ$. Such sets are the raw material of the recent lifting constructions that
improved the kissing numbers in dimensions $25$--$31$: each unit of $|S|$ is worth one or
more spheres in the lifted configuration. This note reports what a sustained computational
attack established about $\max|S|$ and about the structure of the largest known examples.
The headline is a certified ladder $496 \le \max|S| \le 837$; the interesting part is
\emph{why} the ladder is stuck where it is, and a fairly complete structural picture of the
$496$-element sets, which turn out to live inside a Turyn decomposition
$\mathbb{R}^{24} = B_1 \perp B_2 \perp B_3$ with $\Lam \cap B_i \cong \sqrt{2}E_8$, and to be
governed by a $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$ of ``channels'' carrying $8\times 8$ rook grids.
Every claim below carries a verification label; the accompanying archive lets you
re-check the ones marked as attached.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and verification status}

\subsection{The object}

Scale $\Lam$ so that minimal vectors have norm $32$. Then $\ip{v}{w} \in \{0,\pm 8,\pm 16,\pm 32\}$
for $v,w \in C$, $|C| = 196560$, and $\ip{v}{w} = 16$ is exactly the $60^\circ$ condition.
Let $G$ be the \emph{conflict graph} on $C$ with edges at inner product $+16$. Then $G$ is
$4600$-regular, and $\Co = \mathrm{Aut}(\Lam)$ acts arc-transitively on it. A $60^\circ$-free
set is precisely an independent set of $G$, and we write $\alpha = \alpha(G) = \max |S|$.

Throughout, $\Co$ acts on $C$ with rank $7$: the orbitals on ordered pairs are the seven
inner-product classes, with valencies
\[
  (v_{32},v_{16},v_8,v_0,v_{-8},v_{-16},v_{-32}) \;=\; (1,\,4600,\,47104,\,93150,\,47104,\,4600,\,1),
\]
so $C$ carries a commutative $6$-class association scheme. This scheme is the substrate of
everything in Section~2.

the exact rational dual certificate for $\alpha \le 837$; the orbital numbering that certificate is

\section{The bounds ladder, $496 \le \alpha \le 837$}

\subsection{The lower end: $\alpha \ge 496$}\label{sec:lower}

A $496$-element $60^\circ$-free set is attached as an explicit integer matrix, together with a
verifier. \CA{} The set itself is not new: it is one of those found by Ma et al.
(arXiv:2511.13391), and only the coordinate realisation differs here, by a permutation of the
$24$ coordinates.

Its Gram matrix has off-diagonal entries only in $\{-32,-8,0,8\}$, with multiplicities
$496 : 75008 : 95008 : 75008$ over the $496\cdot 495$ ordered off-diagonal pairs. In particular
it contains no pair at $+16$ (independence) and, as a bonus that was not asked for, no pair at
$-16$ either; the $496$ antipodal pairs say $S = -S$. By shape, $S$ consists of $258$ vectors of
type $(\pm 2^8, 0^{16})$, $232$ of type $(\mp 3, \pm 1^{23})$ and $6$ of type
$(\pm 4, \pm 4, 0^{22})$. The second moment $\sum_{v \in S} vv^{\mathsf T}$ has spectrum
$\{576^8, 640^4, 704^8, 768^4\}$, i.e.\ $32\cdot\{18^8,20^4,22^8,24^4\}$: $S$ spans
$\mathbb{R}^{24}$ but is not a tight frame.

There are at least $64$ such sets, each maximal; all $64$ are attached and the verifier
confirms they are distinct. \CA{} That they are moreover pairwise inequivalent under $\Co$
is established in Section~3.6 by a computation whose certificate is not attached: the
verifier separates them only into five tightness-fingerprint classes. \CH{}
\emph{Falsified by:} a $497$-element independent set. Nothing found one; see Section~2.7.

\subsection{Every two-point method gives exactly $9360/11$}\label{sec:twopoint}

This is the sharpest negative result in the note, and it is completely explicit, so we give the
data in full. \CA

The scheme's first eigenmatrix $P$ --- rows indexed by the seven common eigenspaces
$E_0,\dots,E_6$, columns by the classes $32,16,8,0,-8,-16,-32$ --- is
\[
P \;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 4600 & 47104 & 93150 & 47104 & 4600 & 1\\
1 & 2300 & 11776 & 0 & -11776 & -2300 & -1\\
1 & 1000 & 1024 & -4050 & 1024 & 1000 & 1\\
1 & 350 & -704 & 0 & 704 & -350 & -1\\
1 & 76 & -320 & 486 & -320 & 76 & 1\\
1 & -10 & 16 & 0 & -16 & 10 & -1\\
1 & -20 & 64 & -90 & 64 & -20 & 1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
m \;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\ 24\\ 299\\ 2576\\ 17250\\ 95680\\ 80730
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $m_r = \dim E_r$; note $\sum_r m_r = 196560$. Column $2$ of $P$ is the spectrum of the
conflict graph: eigenvalues $4600, 2300, 1000, 350, 76, -10, -20$ with those multiplicities.

\paragraph{Ratio (Hoffman) bound.} With $k = 4600$ and $\lambda_{\min} = -20$,
\[
  \alpha \;\le\; \frac{N(-\lambda_{\min})}{k - \lambda_{\min}}
  \;=\; \frac{196560 \cdot 20}{4620} \;=\; \frac{9360}{11} \;=\; 850.\overline{90}\,,
  \qquad \alpha \le 850 .
\]

\paragraph{Association-scheme (Delsarte) LP.} Maximise $\sum_i a_i$ over inner distributions with
$a_{32} = 1$, $a_{16} = 0$, $a \ge 0$, $aQ \ge 0$ (where $Q = N P^{-1}$). Exact vertex enumeration
over rationals gives optimum $9360/11$, attained at the unique vertex
\[
  a^{*} = \Bigl(1,\;0,\;\tfrac{2944}{11},\;\tfrac{3450}{11},\;\tfrac{2944}{11},\;0,\;1\Bigr)
  \quad\text{(classes $32,16,8,0,-8,-16,-32$).}
\]
The optimal dual is
\[
  \beta \;=\; \Bigl(0,\;\tfrac{116}{231},\;\tfrac{17}{77},\;\tfrac{37}{462},\;
                    \tfrac{8}{385},\;\tfrac{1}{462},\;0\Bigr),
\]
and one checks by hand that $F(i) := \sum_r \beta_r Q_{ir} = -1$ on \emph{every} allowed class
$i \in \{-32,-16,-8,0,8\}$, whence $\sum_i a_i \le 1 + \sum_r \beta_r m_r = 9360/11$. This $\beta$
is not an accident: it is exactly $\beta_r = (\lambda_r - \lambda_{\min})/(k - \lambda_{\min})$,
i.e.\ Hoffman's multiplier vector. The LP cannot beat the ratio bound because its optimal dual
\emph{is} the ratio bound's certificate, and the sign constraints $a \ge 0$ are never active.

\paragraph{Lattice-free Delsarte LP.} Take the allowed cosine set
$T = \{-1,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac14,0,\tfrac14\}$ (i.e.\ forbid $\cos 60^\circ = \tfrac12$) and run the
classical spherical-code LP in $\mathbb{R}^{24}$ with Gegenbauer polynomials normalised by
$G_k(1) = 1$. The exact optimum is $9360/11$ at every truncation degree $\ge 4$; the optimum is
already attained by the degree-$4$ polynomial
\[
  f(t) \;=\; \tfrac{4992}{11}\,(t+1)\,t\,\bigl(t+\tfrac14\bigr)\bigl(t-\tfrac14\bigr),
  \qquad f(-\tfrac12) = -\tfrac{234}{11} < 0,\quad f \equiv 0 \text{ on } T\setminus\{-\tfrac12\},
\]
with Gegenbauer coefficients $f_0 = 1$, $f_1 = 24$, $f_2 = 5083/77$, $f_3 = 4416/11$,
$f_4 = 27600/77$ and $f(1)/f_0 = 9360/11$.

\paragraph{Theta functions and the $\pm 16$ variant.} Symmetry-reduced Lov\'asz $\vartheta$
(drop $a \ge 0$) and Schrijver $\vartheta'$ (keep it) both equal $9360/11$; so the sign
constraints buy nothing. Forbidding \emph{both} $+16$ and $-16$ --- a strictly stronger
combinatorial condition, which the record set happens to satisfy --- also gives exactly
$9360/11$, both with and without the antipodality constraint $a_{-32} = 1$. Entrywise
nonnegativity is likewise inert.

\paragraph{Why they all coincide, and what would change it.} The multiplicities of
$E_0,\dots,E_5$ are $1, 24, 299, 2576, 17250, 95680$, which are exactly
$\dim \mathrm{Harm}_k(S^{23})$ for $k = 0,\dots,5$. That is the $11$-design property of $C$
showing up: those six idempotents are literally the Gegenbauer constraints of degree $\le 5$,
so the scheme LP and the lattice-free LP see the same inequalities there. The seventh idempotent
$E_6$ has dimension $80730$, which is \emph{not} a harmonic space --- $\dim \mathrm{Harm}_6(S^{23})
= 457470$, and $E_6$ is a proper $\Co$-constituent of it. It is the only constraint in the scheme
LP that knows anything about the Leech lattice beyond its $11$-design property, and
\emph{it is slack at the optimum}:
\[
  (a^{*}Q)_r = \Bigl(\tfrac{9360}{11},\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,\tfrac{2152800}{11}\Bigr),
  \qquad \beta_6 = 0 .
\]
So no two-point method can do better than $850$ here, and improving the two-point rung is not a
matter of finding a cleverer polynomial. A three-point relaxation is the first one that sees the
lattice at all.

\emph{Falsified by:} an error in the intersection numbers of the scheme (they are re-derived from
the vector list, and the scheme axioms plus six standard identities are re-checked on load), or
an arithmetic error in the dual above, which is short enough to check by hand.

\subsection{The three-point bound: $\alpha \le 837$}\label{sec:sdp3}

\CA{} --- the exact rational dual certificate is in the archive, and the checker there re-derives
every coefficient matrix it needs from the regenerated lattice, taking nothing from the solver that
produced it.

\paragraph{The relaxation.} Fix $x \in C$. Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the set of orbits of
$\mathrm{Stab}_{\Co}(x)$ on $C \times C$; equivalently, the $\Co$-orbits of ordered triples with a
marked first point. There are exactly $D = 148$ of them, distributed over the pair classes as
$7, 25, 27, 30, 27, 25, 7$. For an independent set $S$ define
\[
  \lambda_u = \#\{(a,b,c) \in S^3 : (a,b,c) \in O_u\},
  \qquad x_u = \frac{\lambda_u}{|S| \cdot \mathrm{size}_u},
\]
where $\mathrm{size}_u = \#\{(y,z) : (x,y,z) \in O_u\}$. Imposing $S_3$-symmetry
($x_u = x_{u^{\mathsf T}} = x_{\mathrm{swap}(u)}$) leaves $43$ free variables. The constraints are:
$M_1 = \sum_u x_u B_u \succeq 0$; $M_2 = \sum_u (x_{\mathrm{diag}(k(u))} - x_u) B_u \succeq 0$;
$0 \le x \le 1$; $x_e = 1$; $\sum_k v_k x_{\mathrm{diag}(k)} = |S|$; and $x_u = 0$ whenever a
pairwise class of $u$ is forbidden. The objective is $\sum_k v_k x_{\mathrm{diag}(k)}$. Since
$M_1 + M_2 = \sum_k x_{\mathrm{diag}(k)} A_k$, the two-point constraint of Section~\ref{sec:twopoint}
is implied and the bound can only improve on $850$.

\paragraph{Symmetry reduction.} The span of $\{B_u\}$, closed under left and right multiplication
by the dual idempotents $E_i^{*}$ and the class matrices $A_k$, has dimension exactly $148 = D$:
the Terwilliger algebra $T(x)$ \emph{is} the full centraliser algebra of the point stabiliser.
This is worth stating carefully, because the span of the $147$ nonempty triple-class matrices
$E_i^{*}A_kE_j^{*}$ is one dimension smaller and is \emph{not} closed under multiplication --- the
Terwilliger algebra is not spanned by the triple-class matrices, and the SDP must live in the full
$148$-dimensional algebra. The dimension count was done by row-reduction over a large prime field,
which gives $\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_p} \le \mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}} \le D$ and hence pins the
value exactly.

The reason $148$ exceeds $147$ is a genuine piece of lattice structure: exactly one triple-class
cell splits into two $\Co$-orbits, namely the most symmetric one, three mutually orthogonal minimal
vectors, with $p^0_{00} = 43164 = 42240 + 924$. So $\Co$ is \emph{not} transitive on ordered
triples with prescribed pairwise classes, and the two orthogonal-triple flavours are exactly the
information a two-point method cannot express.

\paragraph{The left-regular-representation trick.} The $B_u$ span a semisimple matrix
$*$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ (the orbitals are closed under transposition), and $\mathbb{C}^{C}$ is a
faithful $\mathcal{A}$-module containing every irreducible $\mathcal{A}$-module. Hence for
$M \in \mathcal{A}$,
\[
  M \succeq 0 \text{ as a } 196560 \times 196560 \text{ matrix}
  \iff M \succeq 0 \text{ in the left regular representation of } \mathcal{A}.
\]
Writing $c[w,u,t]$ for the structure constants and $\Delta = \mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{size})$, the
matrix $G(x)[w,t] = \mathrm{size}_w \sum_u x_u\, c[w,u,t]$ is symmetric with \emph{integer}
coefficient matrices whenever $x$ is $S_3$-symmetric, and $M(x) \succeq 0 \iff G(x) \succeq 0$,
a $148 \times 148$ test. No numerical block-diagonalisation of the algebra is ever performed and no
irrational number enters the exact side --- which is precisely what makes exact rounding cheap.

\paragraph{Exact rounding.} For rational $Y_1, Y_2 \succeq 0$ and rational multipliers
$\mu, \rho \ge 0$, set $h_q = \mathrm{obj}_q + \langle Y_1, G_q\rangle + \langle Y_2, G^y_q\rangle
+ (\mu^{\mathsf T}Cy)_q - \rho_q$. Weak duality gives
\[
  |S| \;\le\; h_e \;+\; \sum_{q \text{ free}} \rho_q \;+\; \sum_{q \text{ free},\, q \ne e} \max(0, h_q),
\]
every term an exact rational. Validity does not depend on the numerics at all: an inaccurate dual
makes the right-hand side larger, never wrong.

The rounding of $Y_1, Y_2$ is the point worth copying. Rather than round a matrix and then test it
for positive semidefiniteness, the solver's eigendecomposition $Y = \sum_i \lambda_i q_i q_i^{\mathsf T}$
is mapped into the $G$ basis and each term is rounded \emph{separately}: the eigenvalue to a
nonnegative integer $m_i$ and the vector to an integer vector $P_i$, both at a chosen power-of-two
scale, so that $\widehat{Y} = 2^{-T}\sum_i m_i P_i P_i^{\mathsf T}$ with $m_i \ge 0$. This is a
nonnegative combination of rank-one terms, hence positive semidefinite \emph{by construction}, and
no PSD verification of a rounded matrix is ever required. The multipliers $\mu, \rho$ are rounded to
dyadic rationals and clamped at $\ge 0$; both sign conventions are tried and the better bound kept.
The final inequality is evaluated in exact rational arithmetic.

\paragraph{The numbers.} The relaxation's numerical optimum is $837.533238$ (interior-point,
status \texttt{optimal}); the exact rounded dual gives
$837.5358661609\ldots$, a rational with denominator $2^{173}$. Hence
\[
  \boxed{\ \alpha \le 837.\ }
\]
The rounding cost is $1.369$, so the relaxation's own optimum is bracketed in
$[837.5332,\ 837.5359]$; both ends floor to $837$. Note carefully: $837$ is a floor, not the
three-point optimum. Sanity anchors from the identical code path: forbidding nothing returns
$196565 \ge |C|$, and forbidding both $+16$ and $+8$ (i.e.\ $\cos \le 0$) returns exactly $48$,
the orthoplex bound $2 \cdot 24$.

\paragraph{The decisive validity check.} A three-point relaxation is easy to get subtly wrong ---
a normalisation slip produces a beautiful, meaningless number. The check that pins it down is
that the \emph{actual} $496$-element set, and an independent $488$-element set, are exactly feasible
points of the same relaxation: their orbit densities satisfy $0 \le x \le 1$, $x_e = 1$, the
forbidden coordinates vanish, $y \ge 0$, and both $148 \times 148$ blocks are positive semidefinite
under an exact rational symmetric elimination --- and the objective evaluates to $496$ and $488$ on
the nose. Feeding $S = C$ returns $196560$, again on the nose, with the second block identically
zero as the derivation predicts. A wrong normalisation would show up immediately as an
\emph{infeasible} known set. Deliberately perturbing one coordinate of the $496$'s density vector
does make the exact PSD test fail.

\emph{Falsified by:} an independent set of size $838$; or an error in the orbital structure
constants, which are re-derived from the vector list and re-checked at load.

\subsection{Clique cuts are provably inert}\label{sec:cliques}

\paragraph{$\omega(G) = 24$, exactly.} The upper bound is \CA{} and needs no computation. If
$v_1,\dots,v_k$ are minimal vectors with $\ip{v_i}{v_j} = 16$ for $i \ne j$, their Gram matrix is
$16(I_k + J_k)$, whose eigenvalues are $16(k+1)$ once and $16$ with multiplicity $k-1$. This is
positive definite, so the $v_i$ are linearly independent in $\mathbb{R}^{24}$ and $k \le 24$. That
is the whole argument, and it uses no sphere-packing input whatever.

Equality is \CH{}: an explicit $24$-clique is held, not attached. Its Gram matrix is $16(I+J)$
(re-checked in integer arithmetic here), its sum $s$ satisfies $|s|^2 = 9600$ and $\ip{v}{s} = 400$
for every member, the $24$ vectors are a basis of $\mathbb{R}^{24}$, and by shape they are three
vectors of type $(\pm 4,\pm 4,0^{22})$ and $21$ of type $(\pm 2^8, 0^{16})$. Reconstructing one is
a short branch-and-bound in the common neighbourhood of an edge.

A warning for anyone re-deriving this: the natural family $\{4(e_1 + e_i)\}_{i=2}^{24}$ is a
$23$-clique and is \emph{maximal}, because the only real vector at inner product $16$ with all of it
is $(3,1^{23})$, whose coordinate sum $26 \not\equiv 4 \pmod 8$ puts it outside $\Lam$. Maximal is
not maximum here. An exhaustive branch-and-bound in the common neighbourhood of an edge ($891$
vertices, $336$-regular) confirms $\omega = 24$, and a complete enumeration gives the maximal-clique
sizes as exactly $\{8, 12, 15, 17, 23, 24\}$, with $5{,}028{,}032{,}232$ maximal cliques through a
single edge --- the two independent census routes (through an edge, through a triangle) agree
exactly. \CH{} for the census.

\paragraph{The cuts, and why they do nothing.} For a pair $(a,b)$ of non-conflicting vectors and a
clique $K$, the valid pair-conditioned liftings are
\[
  \sum_{v \in K} x_{u(a,b,v)} \;\le\; x_{\mathrm{diag}(i)},
  \qquad
  \sum_{v \in K} \bigl(x_{\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{class}(a,v))} - x_{u(a,b,v)}\bigr) \;\le\; 1 - x_{\mathrm{diag}(i)},
\]
with $i = \mathrm{class}(a,b)$. Adding $225$ such cuts built from maximum cliques leaves the exact
bound at $837$; adding $437$ triangle cuts leaves it at $837$; adding all $662$ leaves it at $837$.
The maximum violation at the uncut optimum, scanned over $1430$ generated cuts, is
$-2.5\times 10^{-14}$: every cut is slack. Both the $496$ and the $488$ remain exactly feasible under
all of them ($80$ of the $662$ tight, the rest strict), which is the validity gate --- a deliberately
corrupted cut is caught by the $496$. \CH{}

The structural reason is a one-line estimate, and it is the useful part of this subsection. The
three-point optimum behaves like a near-product pseudo-density with one-point density
$\sigma = 837.53/196560 \approx 1/234.7$. Conditioned on $(a,b) \in S$, the third-point density at
any single vertex is $O(\sigma)$, so the left-hand side of the first cut is about
$|K|\,\sigma\, x_{\mathrm{diag}(i)}$ and the cut binds only once $|K| \gtrsim 1/\sigma \approx 235$.
But $\omega(G) = 24$ --- an order of magnitude short. Concretely, at the optimum the tightest
instance uses $12\%$ of its right-hand side. Entrywise nonnegativity ($\vartheta^{+}$ strength) is
already implied by the baseline, because the $B_u$ are $0/1$ matrices with disjoint supports.
Clique structure alone could never push a vertex-transitive bound below $|C|/\omega = 8190$ anyway.

Conclusion: at three points, the remaining lever is not combinatorial sparsification. It is either
four-point terms or the split orthogonal-triple cell used directly.

It was not run for exactly one reason: the largest block is $4107$, and the available machine has

\section{Structure of the 496-element sets}

This section is the part most likely to be reusable. Everything in it is an exact computation with
in-run assertions --- integer and rational arithmetic throughout, including Hermite and Smith normal
forms and exact short-vector enumeration --- and is therefore \CH{} unless stated otherwise; the
vector lists it starts from are attached, so any individual claim can be re-derived from the archive
by a reader willing to write the code.

\subsection{The Turyn decomposition}

The setwise stabiliser of the record set in $\Co$ has order $8$ and is elementary abelian $2^3$.
Its eight elements have traces $\{+24, +8, +8, +8, -8, -8, -8, -24\}$; the three trace-$(+8)$
involutions $t_1, t_2, t_3$ satisfy $t_i t_j = -t_k$ (so no two of them generate a Klein group
containing the third). Define $B_i$ to be the $(-1)$-eigenspace of $t_i$. Then
\[
  \mathbb{R}^{24} = B_1 \perp B_2 \perp B_3, \qquad \dim B_i = 8 .
\]
For each $i$, the Gram matrix of $\Lam \cap B_i$, divided by $16$, is an $8$-dimensional even
integral matrix of determinant $1$; by uniqueness of the even unimodular lattice in dimension $8$,
\[
  \Lam \cap B_i \;\cong\; \sqrt{2}\,E_8 ,
\]
confirmed independently by exact short-vector enumeration, which finds $240$ vectors of norm $32$
and $2160$ of norm $64$ in each block. The glue index is
\[
  [\,\Lam : \sqrt2 E_8 \oplus \sqrt2 E_8 \oplus \sqrt2 E_8\,] \;=\; 4096 = 2^{12},
\]
with elementary abelian quotient (Smith form $[2^{12}]$). This is the Turyn construction of $\Lam$
from three copies of $E_8$. Pairwise, $\Lam \cap (B_j \oplus B_k) = \mathrm{Fix}(t_i)$ is a
$16$-dimensional even lattice of determinant $2^8$ with kissing number
$4320 = 3840 + 2\cdot 240$, i.e.\ the Barnes--Wall lattice $BW_{16}$, and
$[\mathrm{Fix}(t_i) : \sqrt2E_8 \oplus \sqrt2E_8] = 16$ with Smith form $[2,2,2,2]$.

The stabiliser is thus concretely the \emph{blockwise sign group}: writing $v = v_1 + v_2 + v_3$ with
$v_i \in B_i$, the eight maps $v \mapsto \varepsilon_1v_1 + \varepsilon_2v_2 + \varepsilon_3v_3$,
$\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}^3$. Its elements are not monomial in the standard Leech coordinates.

Classify $s \in C$ by the multiset of block norms $(|s_1|^2, |s_2|^2, |s_3|^2)$. Only three patterns
occur, because $8$ and $24$ are not norms of $\sqrt2E_8$:

\paragraph{The record lives entirely in the duads.} All $496$ vectors are duads, split
$208 / 144 / 144$ over the three block pairs --- which are exactly the fixed-vector counts of
$t_1, t_2, t_3$ on $S$, since a duad supported on $\{B_j, B_k\}$ is fixed by $t_i$. So the record

\emph{same} three blocks with the \emph{same} $(208,144,144)$ split, as are the $80$ near-miss

There is a pleasing counterpoint. The maximum clique of $G$ from Section~\ref{sec:cliques} consists

carry the big cliques; the two extremal structures of $G$ occupy disjoint strata of the Turyn
decomposition.

Under the blockwise sign group the $196560$ minimal vectors fall into $360$ orbits of size $2$ (the
monads), $2880$ orbits of size $4$ (the duads, each of the form $\{\pm x, \pm y\}$ with $x \perp y$),
and $23040$ orbits of size $8$ (the triads). \emph{Every} triad orbit contains an internal conflict,
so no triad orbit can appear in \emph{any} set invariant under the blockwise $2^3$. That leaves
$3240$ usable orbits of total weight $12240$ --- and the record is $124$ of them.

\subsection{Channels: a $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$}

Take a duad orbit $O = \{\pm x, \pm y\}$ supported on blocks $\{B_j, B_k\}$. Then $x+y$ and $x-y$
are two vectors of norm $64$, one in each block, and they are congruent mod $2\Lam$. Their common
class in $\Lam / 2\Lam$ is the \emph{channel} of the orbit.

Per block, the $2160$ norm-$64$ vectors fall into $135$ classes of $8$ antipodal pairs each, and
each class is an orthogonal frame of that $8$-dimensional block --- the $135$ frames of $E_8$. Of
these, each pair of blocks shares exactly $15$ channels, and --- this is the fact that organises
everything --- \emph{the same $15$ for all three pairs}. Together with $0$ they are closed under
addition in $\Lam/2\Lam$ and span a $4$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_2$-space. So:

\begin{quote}
The $15$ channels are the $15$ points of a $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$: $15$ points, $35$ lines
$\{c, c', c \oplus c'\}$, $15$ planes, and maximum caps of size $8$ (the affine complements of the
planes).
\end{quote}

\paragraph{The rook grid.} A \emph{cell} is a (block-pair, channel) combination; there are
$3 \times 15 = 45$ of them. Within the cell $(\{B_j,B_k\}, c)$, take as rows the $8$ antipodal line
pairs of channel $c$'s frame in $B_j$ and as columns the $8$ in $B_k$; each of the $64$ positions is
one duad orbit. Check: $45 \times 64 = 2880$ orbits, $\times 4 = 11520$ duads.

Two orbits in the same channel conflict if and only if they share a line: sharing a row or column
gives inner product $(\pm 64 + 0)/4 = \pm 16$, which hits $+16$ for one of the sign choices, while
distinct rows and distinct columns give $0$. So within a cell, an independent set is a
\emph{non-attacking rook placement} --- a partial permutation matrix, at most $8$ orbits.

\paragraph{Per-channel independence is a matching condition.} Now stop fixing the pair. A single
channel $c$ has $8$ lines in each of the three blocks; a duad orbit in channel $c$ is exactly an edge
joining a line in one block to a line in another. The $3 \times 64 = 192$ orbits of channel $c$ are
precisely the $192$ edges of the complete tripartite graph $K_{8,8,8}$, and independence within the
channel is exactly the condition that the chosen edges form a \emph{matching}. Since $K_{8,8,8}$ has
$24$ vertices, a channel carries at most $12$ orbits, with equality exactly for a perfect matching.

The two caps together give $15 \times 12 = 180$ orbits, i.e.\ weight $720$ --- which is the ceiling
of Section~\ref{sec:open-slice}, and the reason it is hard to beat. The residual, and the genuinely
hard part, is cross-channel conflict: two orbits in the same pair but different channels conflict
iff \emph{both} their row lines and their column lines are at $60^\circ$ in their respective blocks;
two orbits in different pairs and different channels \emph{never} conflict; and monad-duad conflicts
factor entirely through the plane geometry (a monad's $14$ conflicting lines lie in its own block,
two in each of exactly $7$ channels, and those $7$ always form a plane of $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$). Per
block, both the $120$ channel lines and the $120$ monad lines under $60^\circ$-adjacency form the
strongly regular graph $\mathrm{srg}(120,56,28,24)$ with spectrum $\{56^1, 8^{35}, (-4)^{84}\}$ ---
the $E_8$ root-line graph, whose ratio bound $120 \cdot 4/60 = 8$ is exactly why ``at most $8$ per
block'' keeps recurring.

The record is a union of $124$ orbits of the blockwise sign group, each of size $4$. Its $45$-cell
usage profile is:

so $124 = 3\cdot 12 + 9 \cdot 8 + 2 \cdot 8$ and $|S| = 4 \cdot 124 = 496$. Cell-wise: of the $45$
cells, $9$ carry a full $8$-permutation, $13$ carry $4$, and $23$ are empty;

Two geometric facts about this profile. The seven channels \emph{unused} by the $208$-pair, together

For scale: the record's line budget per block is $72, 88, 88$ of $1080$ available antipodal pairs.
It is not remotely space-constrained; it is constrained by the cross-channel interactions.

\subsection{The three perfect channels are the three frames}

The orthogonality graph on the $248$ antipodal classes of the record --- adjacency at inner
product $0$ --- has $2718$ maximal cliques, with size histogram
$\{8{:}1280,\ 10{:}800,\ 12{:}448,\ 16{:}171,\ 18{:}4,\ 20{:}8,\ 22{:}4,\ 24{:}3\}$.
The three $24$-cliques are pairwise disjoint frames, covering $72$ of the $248$ classes, and they are exactly
the three perfect-matching channels: a perfect matching of $K_{8,8,8}$ in channel $c$ uses all $24$
lines of $c$, its $12$ orbits carry $48$ vectors $=24$ antipodal classes, and those classes are
pairwise orthogonal because distinct matching edges share no line and cross-channel dots vanish. So
\emph{perfect channel $\iff$ $24$-clique in the orthogonality graph $\iff$ frame}.

The three frames are not interchangeable as lattice objects: each generates a rank-$24$ sublattice
of index $2^{24}$, but two of them have Smith form $[2^4, 4^{10}]$ and the third has $[4^{12}]$.

\subsection{The plateau: $64$ maximal sets, and the moves between them}\label{sec:plateau}

Call $v \notin S$ \emph{free} if $S \cup \{v\}$ is still $60^\circ$-free, and define the
\emph{tightness} of $v$ to be $\#\{s \in S : \ip{v}{s} = 16\}$. A set is maximal iff it has no free
vertex; and since a $(k,k+1)$-swap needs a vertex of tightness $\le k$, a minimum tightness of $4$
rules out all swaps with $k \le 3$ at a stroke.

Six pairwise commuting plateau moves exist --- four of size $(12,12)$ and two of size $(80,80)$ ---
all simultaneously applicable, generating a $6$-cube of $2^6 = 64$ independent $496$-element sets.
Every one of the $64$ has: zero free vertices, no vertex of tightness $1$, $2$ or $3$, minimum
tightness exactly $4$ with exactly $80$ tightness-$4$ vertices, and the identical Gram histogram.
They fall into exactly $5$ tightness-fingerprint classes of sizes $16/16/16/8/8$. Each is
independently a certificate for $\alpha \ge 496$. \CA{} --- the $64$ vector lists are attached.

The $64$ are pairwise inequivalent under $\Co$, and fall into $8$ isometry classes of size $8$: no
plateau move is realised by a Leech automorphism, and seven of the $63$ non-trivial sets are
congruent copies of the record in $\mathbb{R}^{24}$ that no element of $\Co$ reaches. The proof is an
exhaustive individualisation-refinement enumeration of all Gram-graph isomorphisms (no pruning can
miss one, because the colours are shared across the disjoint union), each extended to its unique
orthogonal map of $\mathbb{R}^{24}$ and tested for preservation of $\Lam$ on all $196560$ minimal
vectors; five positive controls, including images overlapping the record in as few as four vectors,
are correctly recognised. \CH{}

\paragraph{The moves are channel rewirings.} This is the structurally decisive statement. The $80$
tightness-$4$ vertices of every plateau set are $20$ duad orbits confined to the three frame
channels: the full \emph{complementary} perfect matching of one frame channel ($12$ orbits) plus
four one-pair orbits in each of the other two. Every conflict with $S$ is a rook-row collision --- a
tightness-$4$ orbit conflicts with exactly the two $S$-orbits sharing one of its two rows, which is
exactly why the minimum tightness is $4 = 2$ orbits $\times\ 2$ vectors. There are no cross-channel
conflicts anywhere in $S$ together with its tightness-$4$ vertices.

Each $(12,12)$ move is an alternating $6$-cycle row-rotation inside one frame channel, and the four
of them compose to a frame-for-frame swap. Each $(80,80)$ move removes \emph{all} $20$ orbits of a
\emph{pair} of channels $\{c, c \oplus c_{F}\}$ and installs a fresh $20$-orbit wiring. In every
case the $45$-cell usage profile is \emph{exactly preserved}. The moving parts of the whole
$64$-set plateau are precisely the three pairs of channels swapped by the translation
$c \mapsto c \oplus c_{F}$ in $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$; the nine one-pair channels and the empty channel
never move.

The consequence is worth stating in isolation: \emph{the cell profile is an invariant of the entire
plateau, not merely of the record.} Local search never changes how much each cell carries, only
which matching realises it. Any $497$-element duad set must therefore break the profile itself.

\subsection{Refuted hypotheses}

These are the tempting guesses that turned out to be false. All \CH{}, all verified negatives.

\begin{tightenum}
\item \textbf{The set is not all duads.} It is $496$ of $11520$; per block pair it holds $208$ or
  $144$ of $3840$. The duads are very far from independent --- the duads on a single line form a
  $16$-clique, and there are $20160$ rook conflicts, $322560$ same-pair cross-channel conflicts and
  $23040$ cross-pair same-channel conflicts among duad orbits. (Cross-pair cross-channel conflicts
  number exactly $0$.)
\item \textbf{The set is not the set of minimal vectors of the lattice it generates.} The lattice
  $\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}$ has rank $24$ and index $16$ in $\Lam$ with quotient
  $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$, and it equals $\mathrm{Fix}(t_1) + \mathrm{Fix}(t_2) + \mathrm{Fix}(t_3)$, the
  sum of the three $BW_{16}$ pair lattices. Its minimal vectors number $12240$ --- all $720$ monads
  and all $11520$ duads. So $11744$ minimal vectors of $\langle S \rangle$ lie outside $S$,
  including all $80$ tightness-$4$ vertices. The quotient has exponent $2$, so $2\Lam \subset
  \langle S\rangle$ and membership is a mod-$2\Lam$ condition.
\item \textbf{The class set is not a union of cosets.} The $248$ antipodal classes of $S$ occupy
  $248$ \emph{distinct} classes of $\Lam/2\Lam$ (forced: $x \pm y \in 2\Lam$ would need
  $|x\pm y|^2/4 \ge 32$), spanning a $20$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace. A complete test over
  all $247$ candidate translations shows the stabiliser $\{t : \text{class set} + t = \text{class
  set}\}$ is trivial. (By contrast the \emph{channel} set does have stabiliser of order $2$,
  generated by the empty channel, and no channel class is itself a class of an $S$-vector.)
\item \textbf{The octad incidence of the frames is not the Golay code.} Fix a frame; each of the
  $224$ classes of $S$ outside it is orthogonal to exactly $8$ of the frame's $24$ classes, giving a
  $24$-bit mask. Spanning these masks over $\mathbb{F}_2$ gives, for two of the three frames, a
  self-complementary $[24,7]$ code --- $128$ words, weight distribution
  $\{0{:}1, 8{:}39, 12{:}48, 16{:}39, 24{:}1\}$ --- and for the third a self-complementary
  $[24,9]$ code with $512$ words and weights $\{0{:}1,4{:}12,8{:}135,12{:}216,16{:}135,20{:}12,24{:}1\}$.
  The Golay code is $[24,12,8]$ with $4096$ words and $759$ octads; neither of these is it, and the
  third even has weight-$4$ words, which a minimum-distance-$8$ code cannot. (This is the same
  asymmetry as the Smith-form split above: two frames behave alike, the third does not.)
\item \textbf{The $248 = \dim E_8$ numerology is dead.} $496 = 2^4 \cdot 31$ is a perfect number and
  $248 = \dim E_8$, which invites a $31$-fold or $E_8$-Lie symmetry. But
  $|\Co| = 8315553613086720000 = 2^{22}\cdot 3^9 \cdot 5^4 \cdot 7^2 \cdot 11 \cdot 13 \cdot 23$,
  and $31 \nmid |\Co|$. By Lagrange no element of order $31$, and no subgroup of order divisible by
  $31$, exists in $\Co$; no such symmetry of a $\Co$-stable object can exist. The natural
  factorisation is $496 = 4 \cdot 124$ with $124 = 3\cdot12 + 9\cdot8 + 2\cdot8$, and $248$ is just
  $496/2$. The genuine $E_8$ content of the structure is real but elsewhere:
  $\Lam \cap B_i \cong \sqrt2 E_8$, the $135$ $E_8$ frames per block, and the $E_8$ root-line
  strongly regular graph above.
\end{tightenum}

Two more negatives worth recording. The $\pm 16$-free two-point LP is \emph{not} below $850.9$ ---
it is exactly $9360/11$, as Section~\ref{sec:twopoint} showed. And the $208$ on the largest pair is
not even maximal within its own pair: the single-pair duad graph is $252$-regular, its ratio bound
is about $282$, there exist pair duads with zero conflicts against the $208$, and the true
single-pair optimum is $64$ orbits $= 256$ vectors against the record's $52$ orbits $= 208$. It is
the cross-pair constraints that pin the record down, not the within-pair ones.

\section{Open questions, ranked}

\subsection{Is $496$ maximum in the duad model?}

The finite model is small and completely explicit: $2880$ duad orbits plus $360$ monad orbits, with
the rook condition per cell, the $K_{8,8,8}$ matching condition per channel, the cross-channel rule
of Section~3.3, and the plane-local monad rule. Maximise total weight. The record is $124$ orbits;
the question is whether $125$ exists.

Tractability: the local structure is all proved. Within a single pair, the maximum on a channel
subset $\mathcal{C}$ is exactly $8 \cdot (\text{largest }\mathrm{PG}(3,2)\text{-cap inside }
\mathcal{C})$ --- every single channel gives $8$, every pair of channels $16$, every \emph{line}
$\{c,c',c\oplus c'\}$ gives $16$ (not $24$), every non-collinear triple $24$, every plane $32$, the
whole pair $64$. Monads are provably scarce: the exact ``kill function'' $u(k) = 14, 26, 36, 44, 50,
54, 56, 56$ (the number of block lines destroyed by an independent $k$-set of one block's monads,
achieved inside a single plane) forces full-monad solutions down to weight $432 < 496$. The
symmetry group of the reduced model acts on (channels, pairs) as the full
$\mathrm{GL}(4,2) \times S_3$, order $120960$, so any cap proved for one shape holds for all its
images.

What blocks it is described in \S\ref{sec:open-slice}. My estimate: this is the most likely of the
four to fall, and the right tool is a combinatorial argument on the rook/$\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$ model
rather than a solver --- the object is now genuinely small.

\subsection{Must a maximum set sit on a Turyn frame at all?}

Every $496$-element set anyone here has produced or been given --- the record, the $64$-set plateau,
a thousand further sets from GPU search --- is a duad set on \emph{some} Turyn frame. Nothing proves
that a maximum set must be. If the duad question above were settled at $496$, this would reduce
``$496$ is optimal'' to ``a maximum set admits a blockwise $2^3$''. The only evidence is the
subgroup sweep of Section~\ref{sec:search}: the best set invariant under any group other than the
record's own stabilisers is $402$. That is weak evidence and should be treated as such. I have no
tractability estimate for this one; it is the question I would most like an idea about.

\subsection{The four-point bound}

Shovel-ready and fully scoped (Section~\ref{sec:fourpoint}): orbit data complete, largest block
$4107$, $64$--$128$\,GB, two to four weeks including a sparse-integer rewrite of the certificate
path, expected landing $820$--$830$. This narrows the gap but almost certainly does not close it. If
either of you has access to stronger SDP infrastructure, this is the item that is simply waiting for
a machine.

\subsection{The Turyn-symmetric slice: $496 \le \mathrm{opt} \le 720$}\label{sec:open-slice}

Restrict to sets invariant under the blockwise sign group. The lower end is the record (an
independent union of $124$ orbits). The upper end is $4 \times 15 \times 12 = 720$, from the
per-channel matching cap alone, reproduced independently by a clique-cover LP and by a constraint
solver. \EV{} for the upper end: it rests on solver verdicts, not on an extracted exact certificate.

The obstruction is sharp and worth stating precisely, because it explains why nothing moves.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The flat profile.} Put exactly $4$ orbits in \emph{each} of the $45$ cells and use no
monads. This profile satisfies every quickly-provable local cap: the rook cap ($4 \le 8$), the
$K_{8,8,8}$ matching cap ($4+4+4 = 12$, exactly saturating), every proved per-pair line cap
($\le 16$), plane cap ($\le 32$) and pair total ($\le 64$; a flat pair carries $60$), and the monad
kill cut vacuously. It pays $15 \cdot 12 \cdot 4 = 720$.
\end{quote}

So every linear or Lagrangian relaxation stalls at exactly $720$ --- not approximately, exactly ---
while more than ten hours of exact anytime search never found a $497$. The record's own profile is
nothing like flat: it is the highly uneven $4/4/4$, $8/0/0$, $0/4/4$, $0/0/0$ table of Section~3.4,
$56$ orbits short of the flat $180$. The gap between what a pair can actually do and what a
relaxation can prove is enormous where it matters: with every channel capped at $k$, the best
witness in one pair for $k = 2,3,4$ is $22, 27, 36$ duads while the best proved bound is $30, 45,
60$. A pair genuinely cannot spread thin, and no relaxation tried can see it. Spectral methods do
not help either: the duad-only conflict graph is $254$-regular with $\lambda_{\min} = -18$ exactly,
giving a ratio bound of $190.6$ orbits $=762.4$ weight, and a symmetry-reduced weighted
$\vartheta$ comes out around $1031$, worse than the clique LP.

Moving below $720$ therefore requires refuting a subproblem combinatorially. The natural targets,
with the bound each would certify: pinning the $k=2$ per-pair cap at its witness value gives
$\le 630$; adding $k = 3$ gives $\le 588$; a plane subproblem across all three pairs gives
$\le 560$. A refutation of the full $\ge 497$ instance would be the theorem itself.

One correction to record here, because it is the kind of thing that propagates: an earlier claim
that nine simultaneously perfect channels are infeasible was a mislabelled timeout. Eight
simultaneously perfect channels provably exist (on an affine complement of a plane); ``exactly
eight'' is open. The record has three perfect channels not because four is impossible --- four,
five, six, seven and eight are all feasible --- but as an optimisation trade-off.

\section{Method}

The work was carried out by AI agents operating under my direction, with a two-verifier rule for
every headline claim: no set, family or bound was recorded until two independent implementations,
sharing no code path and regenerating their inputs separately, agreed on it. All certified
statements are computed in exact integer or rational arithmetic --- no floating-point verdict is
ever the last word --- and the headline claims were reimplemented independently in a second
language. That rule earned its keep at least once: a driver bug produced a run of spurious
$497$--$568$ candidates, both verifiers rejected every one of them, and no false record was
logged.
